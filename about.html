About the team

An undergraduate aircraft development program

Colorado Design Build Fly is a student-run engineering team at the University of Colorado Boulder. Each season we design, analyse, manufacture, test and fly a purpose-built aircraft for the AIAA Design/Build/Fly competition.

How we work

We run like a small aerospace firm

Students lead every stage of the program, from requirements through flight test, all outside of class. Members come from aerospace, mechanical and plenty of other majors, first year through graduating senior.

Every stage is reviewed. Flight-test results feed back into analysis, so the aircraft that flies at competition is the product of measured iteration rather than a single guess.

Subteams

Five teams, one aircraft

Each subteam owns a defined part of the aircraft. Pick the one that sounds like you, or try a couple before you settle.

Aerodynamics
01

Aerodynamics

Designs and analyses the lifting surfaces, aircraft dynamics and propulsion system to get the most out of the whole aircraft. Airfoil selection, drag polars, sizing, stability and control, and propeller and motor matching.

XFLR5MATLABFusion 360CFD
You would fit here if You like the maths behind why things fly, and you want to see your numbers proven at the field.
Avionics
02

Avionics

Designs the systems that record flight-test data, return real-time telemetry and operate everything else on the aircraft. Control system, wiring harness, PCB design and radio links.

ArduinoPCB designSolderingTelemetry
You would fit here if You want to build electronics that have to work the first time, at altitude, in wind.
Structures
03

Structures

Designs the internal and external structure of the aircraft for weight and serviceability. Students size the airframe, lay up carbon-fibre skins and bond the aircraft themselves.

Fusion 360FEACompositesLaser cutting
You would fit here if You want to hold the thing you designed and know exactly why it does not break.
Mission
04

Mission

Designs the internal layout and the systems specific to each year's competition missions. Payload bays, release mechanisms, loading procedures and anything the scoring rubric rewards. Works hand in hand with structures.

3D printingMechanismsFusion 360Ground test
You would fit here if You read the rules and immediately start thinking about how to score more points.
Finance & Outreach
05

Finance & Outreach

Tracks program spending, manages sponsor relationships and external funding, and runs the team's social presence, marketing and outreach on campus.

BudgetingSponsor outreachSocial mediaEvents
You would fit here if You want the team to exist next year too, and you are good at talking to people.

Carrying the legacy

Milestones since 1997

Thirty seasons, one aircraft each. A few of the moments that shaped the team.

Est.1997
1997

The first Design/Build/Fly

AIAA launches the competition. A group of CU Boulder seniors enter as a capstone project, and the team has entered every season since.

2012 · Wichita, Kansas

Third place overall

The team's best finish to date, one of three top-five results in its history.

2015 to 2019

Tucson and Wichita

Consistent fly-off appearances through the decade, with the program growing from a senior project into a year-round student team.

2023 · Tucson, Arizona

Ranked fifth all-time

Across the history of the competition, Colorado sat fifth overall in cumulative results.

2026 to 2027

The 31st competition

Five subteams, a new partnership program and a new mark that carries the mountains and the aircraft forward. Fly-off in Tucson, 15 to 18 April 2027.

Where we came from

Every season since the competition began

Design Build Fly started at CU as a senior project. It has since become one of the university's most consistent engineering competition teams, with three top-five finishes and a third place overall in 2012.
