Support here is an engineering investment

Partners fund the materials, travel and workspace behind a student-built aircraft, and get direct access to the engineers who built it. Funding, in-kind support, mentorship and multi-season agreements are all welcome.

Engineering Excellence Fund

Funded by engineering student fees, the EEF backs student projects and organizations across the college. For Colorado DBF it is the foundation of each season's budget, from airframe materials to the trip to the fly-off.

Boom Supersonic

Boom is building Overture, a supersonic airliner, a short drive from campus, and its XB-1 demonstrator broke the sound barrier in January 2025. A Colorado aerospace company backing the next generation of Colorado aerospace engineers.

Atlas Motion Systems

Atlas designs and builds brushless DC motors and actuators for drones and robots, engineered around each customer's program and manufactured at scale. Electric propulsion is the heart of every DBF aircraft, so having a motor company in our corner matters.

In-kind and technical support

Products and services we need

Not every partnership is a cheque. A roll of carbon, a software licence or an afternoon of machining capacity moves the program just as far.

Materials and consumables

  • Carbon-fibre fabric
  • Epoxy resin systems
  • 3D-printing filament
  • Fasteners and hardware kits
  • Storage cabinets
  • Electronics and avionics components

Equipment and manufacturing

  • Laser cutter
  • Large-format 3D printer
  • Desktop CNC mill
  • Portable generator
  • Engineering workstation
  • Trainer aircraft and transmitters

Services and expertise

  • Machining and fabrication capacity
  • Additive manufacturing runs
  • CAD, CFD and FEA software licences
  • Test and measurement equipment
  • Engineering mentorship
  • Workspace and secure storage
Where support goes

Roughly $8,000 a season, spent on hardware and getting there

About a third of the budget is travel to the competition. The rest is manufacturing, electronics and testing: materials, manufacturing supplies, AIAA membership and program costs.

Largest unmet need

A dedicated workspace

The team builds at weekends, when university shops are closed, and has no dedicated storage for airframes, tooling and materials. Solving this is the single highest-leverage contribution a partner can make.

  • Short term · a rented workshop in or around Boulder, roughly $1,000 to $1,500 a month
  • Long term · a purchased tool-shed workshop, about 20 by 14 ft, on a donated or granted plot
