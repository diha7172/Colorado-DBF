Learning to fly, together

Any major, any year, any amount of experience. Here is how to go from interested to building in about a week.

Why join us

What you get out of it

01

Get familiar with engineering

See what an engineering project actually looks like before your senior design class does.

02

Learn the process

Requirements, reviews, reports, deadlines. The whole cycle in one year.

03

Hands-on manufacturing and testing

Foam, balsa, carbon, soldering irons and a field full of wind.

04

Learn how RC airplanes fly

And how to make them fly, which is harder than it sounds at 5,430 feet.

05

Meet and work with a fun community

Sunday afternoons with people who also think planes are cool.

06

Leadership opportunities

Stay for a year and there is a high chance of a lead role the next.

First build night
What to expect

Wherever you are starting from

  • No experience in CAD, electronics or aerodynamics. You will work on building our first prototype and learn at least one of those this semester.
  • Some experience with one of them. You will build prototypes and design items that go onto the competition plane.
  • Other knowledge entirely. Talk to a lead. There is a high chance we need your experience.
Four steps

How to get started

Step 01

Complete the form

One short form. It puts you on the mailing list, starts access to the projects space, and lets you pick which meeting day works for you.

Open the form
Step 02

Join Teams

Join the channels of the subteams you are interested in and add the DBF folder to your OneDrive. Meeting rooms and pinned links live there.

Open Teams
Step 03

Set up your tools

Install the software and knock out any ITLL or Aerospace shop trainings you are missing. Bookmark the pinned links in your subteam channel.

Member resources
Step 04

Show up Sunday at 2

Come to a general meeting and introduce yourself. If you want a bigger role, get in contact with the subteam lead directly.
