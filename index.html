Who we are

A competition club. Every year there are **new rules, new requirements, a new design and a new plane.** We read the rules in August and fly the result in April.

Colorado Design Build Fly is a student-run engineering team at the University of Colorado Boulder. Each season the team designs, analyses, manufactures, tests and flies a purpose-built aircraft for AIAA Design/Build/Fly. Members come from aerospace, mechanical and plenty of other majors, first year through graduating senior, and most arrive with no RC experience at all.

Designed a mile up, in the shadow of the Flatirons

Everything we build is designed, built and test-flown in Boulder, Colorado, at 5,430 feet. The air here is roughly 15 percent thinner than at sea level, so every wing has to make more lift and every propeller has to work harder. When the aircraft flies in Tucson or Wichita, it has already been proven somewhere harder.

  • CU Boulder · home of the Ann and H.J. Smead Department of Aerospace Engineering Sciences, where most of the team studies
  • Since 2003 · CU Boulder aircraft with Buffalo names, from Water Buffalo and Buffalo Wings to H2BuffalO and Ralphie's Revenge
  • Wichita, 2012 · third place overall with H2BuffalO, the team's best finish at the AIAA fly-off
  • Tucson, April 2027 · the 31st competition, and the season we are building for now

Our mission

Where classroom theory meets real deadlines

Competition motivates us, but the point is bigger than a winning plane. DBF is a hands-on environment where students apply what they learn, work in teams and solve practical problems with real hardware.

01

Aerodynamics

Lifting surfaces, aircraft dynamics and propulsion. Airfoil selection, sizing, stability and control, and the mission simulation.
02

Structures & Mission

The airframe, sized for weight and serviceability, plus the payload bays and mechanisms that score points.
03

Avionics & Telemetry

Flight-test data logging, real-time telemetry, wiring, PCB design and control of every moving part on the aircraft.
04

Manufacturing

Foam prototypes, laser-cut and 3D-printed parts, carbon layups, integration, fabrication and the flight test campaign.
05

Finance & Outreach

Program spending, sponsor relationships, external funding and the team's public presence.

The season

One aircraft. Eight months. Every stage reviewed.

Rules drop in late summer. From there it is mission analysis, a proposal, preliminary and critical design reviews, a written report, manufacturing, ground test and flight test, with results fed straight back into the next revision.

  • Late August · info meeting, foam prototype workshops, rules release
  • October · proposal due, preliminary design review
  • December to February · critical design review, build, design report
  • February to April · flight test, refine, compete

Why join us

What you actually get to do

Learn the process of an engineering project

Requirements, design reviews, a scored report and a hard deadline in April. You see the whole cycle in one year, before senior design does it to you.

Hands-on manufacturing and testing

Foam, balsa, carbon fibre, soldering irons, the laser cutter and a field full of wind. You learn how RC airplanes fly by making one fly.

A fun community, and a short path to leading

Sunday afternoons with people who also think planes are cool. Stay a year and there is a high chance of a lead role the next.
